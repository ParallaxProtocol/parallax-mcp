Parallax Wallet runs a full Parallax node inside the app. It downloads and
checks every block itself, and your balance, your history and your sends
all go through it, never through someone else's server. This page covers
where you see it and what you can do with it.

---

## The node bar

The capsule along the bottom of the window shows your node from every page:

- **Its state**: **Syncing** (with a progress bar), **Synced**, **Offline**
  or **Restarting**.
- **The best block**, **its peers** and **how long it has been up**.
- **Connect** or **Disconnect**, to start or stop the node.

When a change in **Settings** needs the node to restart, the bar says
**Restart to apply your changes**, with a button to do it. With Tor on, it
also says how Tor is doing: the node waits for Tor before it connects (see
**Settings → Privacy**).

Click the bar (anywhere but its button) to open it into the **Network**
panel. Its collapse button, or Escape, closes it again.

---

## When the node is offline

While the node is stopped, the panel says **You're not connected**, with
**Connect** to start it. Your addresses and keys still work, but balances,
activity and sending wait for the node. If the last start failed, the bar
and the panel say why, until a start succeeds.

---

## Syncing

On first launch, and after the app has been closed a while, the node
catches up with the network:

- **Looking for peers**: it's connecting to the network. Syncing starts as
  soon as a peer answers.
- **Syncing**: it's downloading and checking the chain, with how many
  blocks are left. The node is ready once it reaches the network's head.

The first sync uses **Snap** sync by default, which gets a usable node in
hours rather than days; see **Settings → Node → Sync mode**. **Keep the
computer awake**, in **Settings → General**, stops it from sleeping while
the node syncs.

## The block clock

Once synced, the panel leads with the **best block**, the newest block your
node has, and the time **since the last block**. Parallax aims for a block
every 10 minutes, but blocks are found at random, so the wait varies a lot.
The clock says how this one stands:

| It says | The wait so far |
|---|---|
| **On schedule** | Up to 10 minutes |
| **Running long** | Up to 30 minutes |
| **Unusually long** | Up to an hour |
| **Very long wait** | Over an hour |

Beside it, **"N% of blocks take longer"** puts the wait in context: even
after 20 minutes, about one block in seven still takes longer. A long wait
on its own isn't a fault.

Under the clock, the **block lane** draws the last two hours of blocks on a
timeline: each block a bar at the moment it was found, as tall as it was
full, and the wait since the last one growing towards **Now**, against a
tick at the 10 minute target.

## Peers, the mempool, the fee and uptime

A row of figures sits under the clock:

| Figure | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **Peers** | How many nodes yours is connected to, split into **inbound** (they connected to you) and **outbound** (you connected to them) |
| **Mempool** | Transactions waiting to go into a block: how many are **pending**, how many are **queued**, and their size |
| **Recommended fee** | The fee to offer now: the smart fee estimate when your node has one, or the suggested gas price while it warms up |
| **Uptime** | How long since the node last started |

Click **Peers** or **Mempool** to open its list.

### Peers

The list shows each peer's direction, client, address and latency, and
filters by **All**, **Inbound**, **Outbound** or **Blocked**. Open a peer to
see its full node ID, enode URL, addresses, network and capabilities, and
to act on it:

- **Disconnect** it.
- **Trust** it: a trusted peer can connect even when every peer slot is
  taken. Only peers with an enode URL can be trusted.
- **Block** it: it's disconnected, and its IP address refused for 1 hour,
  1 day, 1 week, 30 days or permanently.

**Add peer** connects to a node by its enode URL (`enode://…`) or its
`ip:port`. An enode URL is dialed again whenever it drops, even after a
restart; an `ip:port` goes in the node's address book and is dialed first.

**Blocked** lists every blocked address with when its block ends. You can
unblock one, unblock them all, or block an IP address or a subnet by hand
(for example `203.0.113.0/24`).

### Mempool

The list shows up to 200 waiting transactions, searchable by hash or
address:

- **Pending**: ready to go into the next block.
- **Queued**: not minable yet, held back by a gap in its account's nonces
  or too little balance.

Open a transaction to see its hash, sender and recipient, nonce, value,
gas price, gas limit, the most it can pay in fees, its input data and its
size.

## More details

**More details**, at the foot of the panel, opens the rest, a card per
group. The app remembers whether you left it open.

| Group | What's there |
|---|---|
| **Node** | Its sync mode (and whether it's pruned or an archive node), the daemon's version, its node ID, whether it reports to the [status page](https://status.parallaxprotocol.org) and under what name, and snap sync's progress while it runs |
| **Chain** | The difficulty (the work needed to find the next block), the total difficulty (all the work behind the chain) and the head block's hash |
| **Usage** | CPU, memory, the free space on the chain's disk, disk activity, and the data directory |
| **Networking** | Peers out of the most allowed, split by direction and by network (clearnet or onion), download and upload speed, whether other nodes can reach yours, and your node's enode URL |

The free space turns amber as it nears the point where the node stops
itself to protect its database, and red once it's there. That point is
**Settings → Node → Minimum free disk space**; see
[Troubleshooting](/parallax-wallet/troubleshooting#the-node).
